VGA Sync Polarity for 350-Line Displays

Video Graphics Array (VGA) standards utilize the polarity of the horizontal and vertical synchronization signals to communicate the display's vertical resolution to the monitor. This article explains the specific synchronization polarity combination used to signal a 350-line active display mode and outlines how VGA displays interpret these signals.

To indicate a 350-line active display, the VGA interface uses a positive Horizontal Sync (H-Sync) signal combined with a negative Vertical Sync (V-Sync) signal.

The VGA Sync Polarity Mechanism

Original VGA hardware required a reliable, hardware-level method to instruct cathode-ray tube (CRT) monitors to adjust their vertical scanning height automatically. Instead of transmitting complex digital metadata, the IBM VGA standard utilized the electrical polarities (active-high or active-low pulses) of the H-Sync and V-Sync lines.

The standard defines the following polarity combinations for vertical line counts:

  • 350 Lines (EGA Compatibility Mode): Positive H-Sync (+), Negative V-Sync (-)
  • 400 Lines (Text Mode): Negative H-Sync (-), Positive V-Sync (+)
  • 480 Lines (Standard VGA Graphics Mode): Negative H-Sync (-), Negative V-Sync (-)
  • Reserved Mode: Positive H-Sync (+), Positive V-Sync (+)

Purpose of the 350-Line Mode

The 350-line mode was primarily implemented to provide backward compatibility with the Enhanced Graphics Adapter (EGA) standard, such as the 640x350 graphics mode. When an active-high pulse on the horizontal sync line is paired with an active-low pulse on the vertical sync line, the monitor detects the configuration and adjusts its vertical deflection circuitry to properly stretch and center the 350-line image without distortion.
