VGA Pinout: Which Pin Carries the Blue Signal?

This guide identifies the specific pin responsible for the blue analog video signal on a standard 15-pin DE-15 Video Graphics Array (VGA) connector. In addition to pinpointing the exact signal line, this article covers its dedicated ground return pin, signal specifications, and the primary RGB layout found on standard VGA interfaces.

The Blue Video Signal: Pin 3

On a standard female or male DE-15 connector (commonly referred to simply as a VGA connector), Pin 3 carries the analog blue video signal (often designated as PB or RED/GREEN/BLUE).

Like the red (Pin 1) and green (Pin 2) channels, Pin 3 transmits an analog voltage typically ranging from 0 to 0.7 volts peak-to-peak into a 75-ohm load. Variations within this voltage range dictate the intensity of the blue color displayed on the monitor.

The Dedicated Blue Ground: Pin 8

High-frequency analog video signals require shielding and individual ground returns to prevent crosstalk and image ghosting.

  • Signal: Pin 3 (Blue Video)
  • Ground Return: Pin 8 (Blue Ground)

Pin 8 serves as the specific coaxial shield ground return for the blue signal line. When wiring or troubleshooting a VGA cable, continuity between the shield of the blue internal coaxial wire and Pin 8 is critical for signal integrity.

Primary Video Pin Overview

The top row of the three-row DE-15 connector handles the primary color components:

  • Pin 1: Red Video (Analog)
  • Pin 2: Green Video (Analog)
  • Pin 3: Blue Video (Analog)
  • Pin 6: Red Ground
  • Pin 7: Green Ground
  • Pin 8: Blue Ground

If Pin 3 is damaged, bent, or disconnected, the display will lose all blue color information, causing the image to appear heavily tinted with yellow.
