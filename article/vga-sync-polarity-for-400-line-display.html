VGA Sync Polarity for 400-Line Display

In the standard Video Graphics Array (VGA) interface, monitors determine the active vertical resolution of an incoming analog signal by detecting the polarity of its synchronization signals. To signal a 400-line active display, the VGA standard utilizes a negative Horizontal Sync (H-Sync) and a positive Vertical Sync (V-Sync) polarity combination.

The 400-Line Sync Polarity Combination

For a 400-line vertical display mode—most commonly encountered in text-mode interfaces like 720×400 at 70 Hz or 640×400 graphics modes—the sync polarities are:

  • Horizontal Sync (H-Sync): Negative (−)
  • Vertical Sync (V-Sync): Positive (+)

A "negative" polarity indicates that the sync pulse transitions from high (active voltage, roughly +5V TTL) to low (0V ground) for the duration of the sync pulse. A "positive" polarity indicates that the pulse remains at ground (0V) and pulses high (+5V TTL).

How VGA Sync Polarity Signaling Works

During the development of the IBM VGA standard in 1987, multisync cathode-ray tube (CRT) monitors lacked modern digital communication protocols like DDC (Display Data Channel) or EDID (Extended Display Identification Data). To automatically adjust the vertical deflection amplifier and maintain the correct display height across varying resolutions, monitors decoded the polarity combinations of the two hardware synchronization lines.

The original standard defined three primary vertical line counts using these polarities:

Vertical Resolution Horizontal Sync (H-Sync) Vertical Sync (V-Sync) Common Usage
350 Lines Positive (+) Negative (−) EGA compatibility modes (e.g., 640×350, 720×350)
400 Lines Negative (−) Positive (+) Text modes (720×400) and low-res graphics (640×400)
480 Lines Negative (−) Negative (−) Standard VGA graphics mode (640×480)

A positive/positive (+/+) combination was originally reserved, though later VESA standards adapted it for alternative resolutions such as 800×600.

Purpose of Polarity Detection

When a multisync monitor detects a negative H-Sync paired with a positive V-Sync, internal analog circuitry automatically adjusts the vertical scan amplitude. This compensation stretches the 400 active scan lines across the entire physical face of a standard 4:3 CRT screen, preventing letterboxing and ensuring that text and graphics fill the display correctly.
