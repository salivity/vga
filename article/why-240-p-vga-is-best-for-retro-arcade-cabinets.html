Why 240p VGA is Best for Retro Arcade Cabinets

Connecting a modern emulation PC to a vintage arcade cabinet is the ultimate way to access thousands of classic games on original hardware, but doing so properly requires outputting a 240p resolution over Video Graphics Array (VGA). Enthusiasts prize 240p over VGA because it preserves the authentic visual presentation, scanline structure, and motion clarity of original arcade hardware while sending an analog signal that arcade cathode-ray tube (CRT) chassis can safely display without lag or distortion.

Matching the 15 kHz Native Standard

Vintage arcade monitors operate at a 15 kHz horizontal refresh rate, which corresponds to low-resolution video signals around 240p (progressive) or 480i (interlaced). Modern PC graphics cards and flat-panel displays, by contrast, output a minimum of 31 kHz (typically 480p and higher).

Plugging a standard modern PC output into a 15 kHz arcade monitor can physically damage the chassis flyback transformer and deflection circuits. Outputting a 240p signal via customized PC drivers (such as CRT Emudriver) forces the graphics card to drop to a 15 kHz broadcast standard, making the signal electronically compatible with original arcade CRT hardware.

Pure Analog RGB Signal Path

Arcade chassis require an analog RGB video signal alongside synchronization lines (often combined into composite sync, or C-Sync). VGA is fundamentally an analog RGBHV format (Red, Green, Blue, Horizontal sync, Vertical sync).

Because VGA carries analog video natively, it eliminates the need for active digital-to-analog converters (DACs) that introduce input latency, color compression, or scaling artifacts. A 15 kHz VGA output can be piped directly into an arcade monitor through simple, passive breakout boards or dedicated interfaces (such as a J-PAC) that simply combine the H/V sync lines into C-Sync and amplify the signal levels to match arcade voltages.

True Progressive Scanlines

Most classic arcade games—from the Golden Age classics to 1990s fighting games like Street Fighter II—rendered at low progressive resolutions, commonly ranging between 224p and 256p.

When displayed on a 15 kHz CRT:

  • Every active scanline is drawn with blank space between it, producing natural, hardware-generated scanlines.
  • Interlacing flicker is eliminated, producing a sharp, rock-solid image.
  • Software shaders and simulated scanlines are unnecessary, saving GPU resources and delivering authentic light bloom from the physical phosphor grid.

Dynamic Refresh Rates and Native Timing

Original arcade printed circuit boards (PCBs) rarely ran at a flat 60.00 Hz. For example, Mortal Kombat runs at 54.7 Hz, while R-Type runs at 55.0 Hz.

When a PC outputs 240p over VGA paired with arcade-focused emulation software like GroovyMAME, it can dynamically change both the pixel resolution and the exact refresh rate to match the original game board. The arcade monitor syncs directly to these custom timings, resulting in tear-free, stutter-free, zero-lag scrolling that is indistinguishable from running an original arcade PCB.

Uncompromised Motion Clarity

Modern flat panels rely on sample-and-hold display technology, which creates motion blur during fast action. Vintage arcade CRTs illuminate phosphors nearly instantaneously with an electron beam. Running 240p over VGA into an authentic arcade chassis retains this zero-motion-blur characteristic, ensuring fast-moving sprites and scrolling backgrounds remain crystal clear during high-speed gameplay.
