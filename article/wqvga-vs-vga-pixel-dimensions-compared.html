WQVGA vs VGA: Pixel Dimensions Compared

This article provides a direct comparison between Wide Quarter Video Graphics Array (WQVGA) and standard Video Graphics Array (VGA) display resolutions. It breaks down their specific pixel dimensions, aspect ratios, and total pixel counts to illustrate how the wide-screen variant compares in physical resolution to the traditional display baseline.

Video Graphics Array (VGA) Dimensions

Introduced by IBM in 1987, standard VGA has a fixed resolution of 640 × 480 pixels.

  • Width: 640 pixels
  • Height: 480 pixels
  • Aspect Ratio: 4:3
  • Total Pixel Count: 307,200 pixels (0.3 megapixels)

VGA served as a computer display standard for decades and remains a foundational reference point for resolution naming conventions.

Wide Quarter Video Graphics Array (WQVGA) Dimensions

Standard Quarter VGA (QVGA) is exactly one-quarter the total pixel area of VGA, measuring 320 × 240 pixels (half the width and half the height). WQVGA takes the vertical resolution of QVGA and expands the horizontal axis to fit widescreen formats.

Because "widescreen" can refer to multiple aspect ratios, WQVGA does not refer to a single set of dimensions. Common WQVGA implementations include:

  • 400 × 240 pixels (5:3 aspect ratio) – Common in older smartphones and handheld devices.
  • 428 × 240 or 432 × 240 pixels (Roughly 16:9 aspect ratio) – Standard true widescreen variants.
  • 480 × 272 pixels (Approximately 16:9) – Popularized by portable gaming devices like the Sony PlayStation Portable (PSP).

Direct Comparison: WQVGA vs. VGA

When comparing standard VGA (640 × 480) to the most common WQVGA standard (400 × 240):

  • Vertical Resolution: VGA has exactly double the vertical pixel count of WQVGA (480 pixels versus 240 pixels).
  • Horizontal Resolution: VGA is significantly wider than WQVGA in absolute pixels (640 pixels versus 400 pixels), despite WQVGA having a wider proportional aspect ratio.
  • Total Screen Area (Pixel Density):
    • VGA contains 307,200 pixels.
    • A 400 × 240 WQVGA display contains 96,000 pixels.
    • A 480 × 272 WQVGA display contains 130,560 pixels.

In terms of overall visual information, WQVGA provides roughly 31% to 42% of the total pixels found in a standard VGA display. While WQVGA offers a wider field of view proportionally (16:9 or 5:3 versus VGA's 4:3), it features lower dimensions across both axes when compared to base VGA.
