Understanding VGA CRT Phosphor Persistence

Standard office-grade Video Graphics Array (VGA) cathode ray tube (CRT) monitors rely on medium-short persistence phosphors to balance image stability with motion clarity. Designed primarily for productivity tasks like document editing and basic graphic display at refresh rates between 60 Hz and 85 Hz, these displays utilize the P22 phosphor trio to prevent image ghosting while minimizing noticeable flicker.

Persistence Classification and Decay Rates

CRT phosphors are classified by their decay time, defined as the time required for light emission to drop to 10% of its initial peak luminance once the electron beam excitation stops.

Standard office VGA monitors fall into the medium-short persistence category:

  • Decay to 10% Luminance: Typically occurs between 1 millisecond and 10 milliseconds.
  • Decay to 1% Luminance: Generally concludes within 10 to 30 milliseconds.

This specific duration ensures that a drawn pixel naturally fades before the electron beam returns in the next refresh cycle, preventing double images.

The Standard Phosphor: The P22 Designation

Office-grade color VGA monitors almost universally employed the P22 phosphor screen standard (standardized by the Electronic Industries Alliance, or EIA). P22 is a multi-component phosphor system comprising separate materials for each primary color:

  • Blue: Silver-doped zinc sulfide (\(\text{ZnS:Ag}\)), which features a very short persistence (decaying in tens of microseconds).
  • Green: Copper- and aluminum-doped zinc cadmium sulfide (\(\text{ZnCdS:Cu,Al}\)) or gold/copper-doped zinc sulfide (\(\text{ZnS:Cu,Au}\)), exhibiting medium-short persistence (roughly 1 to 5 milliseconds).
  • Red: Europium-doped yttrium oxysulfide (\(\text{Y}_2\text{O}_2\text{S:Eu}\)) or yttrium oxide (\(\text{Y}_2\text{O}_3\text{Eu}\)), exhibiting medium-short persistence (around 1 to 2 milliseconds).

Because the individual phosphors within the P22 matrix possess slightly different decay curves, overall screen persistence is effectively anchored by the green and red components, giving the display its aggregate medium-short characteristic.

Alignment with Standard Refresh Rates

Office VGA standards originally targeted a 60 Hz vertical refresh rate (yielding roughly 16.6 milliseconds per frame) and later progressed to ergonomic office standards such as 72 Hz, 75 Hz, and 85 Hz (11.7 milliseconds per frame).

The medium-short persistence of the P22 phosphor set corresponds directly to these frame windows:

  • Elimination of Motion Smear: If persistence exceeded 16.6 milliseconds, scrolling black text on a white background or moving a cursor would result in visible streaking and smearing.
  • Flicker Mitigation: If persistence were extremely short (under 10 microseconds, such as radar-grade or P16 phosphors), the human eye would perceive unbearable strobe-like flicker at 60 Hz because the screen would remain dark for the majority of the frame interval.

Contrast with Specialized Monitors

Unlike monochrome data terminals that used long-persistence phosphors (such as P39 or P7, which lingered for hundreds of milliseconds to eliminate flicker at low 30 Hz to 50 Hz refresh rates), office VGA CRTs were general-purpose computer displays. The medium-short persistence of the P22 phosphor provided the exact threshold necessary to render clean text, legible scrolling documents, and responsive user interfaces without distracting visual artifacts.
