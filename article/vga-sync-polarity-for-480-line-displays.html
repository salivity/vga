VGA Sync Polarity for 480-Line Displays

Standard Video Graphics Array (VGA) displays rely on the polarities of horizontal and vertical synchronization pulses to communicate resolution geometry to the monitor. For a standard 480-line active display, both the Horizontal Sync (H-Sync) and Vertical Sync (V-Sync) lines must be set to negative polarity. This combination serves as a hardware-level signal instructing the display to scale and center an active vertical resolution of 480 lines.

When IBM introduced the VGA standard in 1987, modern bidirectional display communication protocols like Display Data Channel (DDC) did not exist. Monitors needed an immediate, analog-compatible method to determine whether to adjust vertical raster sizing for different standard display formats. IBM designed the VGA controller to encode this information directly into the active pulse states (positive or negative polarity) of the synchronization lines.

The sync polarity standard defines vertical line counts using the following combinations:

  • Negative H-Sync, Positive V-Sync: Indicates a 400-line active vertical display, typically used in text modes like 720x400.
  • Positive H-Sync, Negative V-Sync: Indicates a 350-line active vertical display, maintaining backward compatibility with Enhanced Graphics Adapter (EGA) modes.
  • Negative H-Sync, Negative V-Sync: Indicates a 480-line active vertical display, standard for 640x480 graphic modes running at a nominal 60 Hz refresh rate.
  • Positive H-Sync, Positive V-Sync: Originally reserved, but later adopted by VESA standards for alternate higher-resolution modes, such as 1024x768.

In the case of a 480-line display, setting both lines to negative polarity means that the sync pulses are active-low. The signal remains high during the active line or frame duration and drops low during the sync pulse interval. Monitor circuitry reads these active-low transitions to activate the correct deflection circuitry, ensuring that a full 480 lines of active video are displayed without geometric distortion or vertical stretching.
