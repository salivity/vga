Exact Refresh Rate of 640x480 VGA at 25.175 MHz

While the standard 640x480 Video Graphics Array (VGA) resolution is commonly referred to as a 60 Hz display mode, its actual vertical refresh rate is not an even 60 Hz. Driven by an industry-standard 25.175 MHz pixel clock, the exact mathematical refresh rate is approximately 59.9405 Hz (or precisely 5,035/84 Hz). This article details the full horizontal and vertical timing parameters used by the standard VGA specification to calculate this precise frequency.

VGA Timing Specifications

To calculate the refresh rate, you must account for the blanking intervals (front porch, sync pulse, and back porch) in addition to the visible display area.

Horizontal Timings (Pixels)

  • Visible Area: 640 pixels
  • Front Porch: 16 pixels
  • Sync Pulse: 96 pixels
  • Back Porch: 48 pixels
  • Total Horizontal Pixels (\(H_{\text{total}}\)): 800 pixels

Vertical Timings (Lines)

  • Visible Area: 480 lines
  • Front Porch: 10 lines
  • Sync Pulse: 2 lines
  • Back Porch: 33 lines
  • Total Vertical Lines (\(V_{\text{total}}\)): 525 lines

The Exact Refresh Rate Calculation

  1. Calculate Total Pixels Per Frame: \[\text{Total Pixels} = H_{\text{total}} \times V_{\text{total}} = 800 \times 525 = 420,000 \text{ pixels}\]

  2. Calculate Horizontal Scan Rate: \[\text{Horizontal Frequency} = \frac{\text{Pixel Clock}}{H_{\text{total}}} = \frac{25,175,000 \text{ Hz}}{800} = 31,468.75 \text{ Hz (31.46875 kHz)}\]

  3. Calculate Vertical Refresh Rate: \[\text{Vertical Refresh Rate} = \frac{\text{Pixel Clock}}{\text{Total Pixels}} = \frac{25,175,000 \text{ Hz}}{420,000}\]

Simplifying the fraction: \[\frac{25,175,000}{420,000} = \frac{25,175}{420} = \frac{5,035}{84} \text{ Hz}\]

In decimal form, this evaluates to: \[\approx 59.94047619... \text{ Hz}\]

Significance of 59.94 Hz

This exact timing was chosen during the design of the IBM VGA standard to closely align with the NTSC color television field rate (\(60 / 1.001 \approx 59.94006 \text{ Hz}\)). This alignment simplified video capture, broadcast conversion, and timing synchronization with existing display hardware while operating within standard CRT beam-deflection limits.
