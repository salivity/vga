What Is Ultra Video Graphics Array (UVGA)?

Ultra Video Graphics Array, commonly abbreviated as UVGA, refers to a specific computer display resolution standard defined as 1280 × 960 pixels. Introduced as a true 4:3 aspect ratio display standard, UVGA was developed to scale the original Video Graphics Array (VGA) standard while maintaining proper geometry for standard CRT monitors and traditional display panels. This article covers the specifications, origin, and significance of the UVGA display standard.

UVGA offers a native resolution of 1280 horizontal pixels by 960 vertical pixels, resulting in an exact 4:3 aspect ratio. This configuration yields a total of 1,228,800 pixels. Because it doubles both the horizontal (640) and vertical (480) pixel counts of standard VGA, UVGA provides precisely four times the display area of standard VGA. For this reason, the standard is also frequently referred to as Quad-VGA or SXGA- (SXGA minus).

The specification gained relevance primarily as an alternative to Super Extended Graphics Array (SXGA). While SXGA offered a resolution of 1280 × 1024 pixels, it utilized a 5:4 aspect ratio. On standard 4:3 cathode-ray tube (CRT) monitors of the era, displaying an SXGA signal resulted in either distorted, non-square pixels or geometric stretching. UVGA solved this issue by reducing the vertical pixel count from 1024 to 960, allowing software and operating systems to run at high resolutions with perfectly square pixels on 4:3 screens.

In modern computing, UVGA is considered a legacy resolution, largely superseded by widescreen standards such as Full HD (1920 × 1080) and higher. However, the 1280 × 960 resolution remains standard in specific retro-computing environments, older digital camera sensors, industrial imaging devices, and legacy flight simulators that require precise 4:3 scaling.
