iPhone 4 Retina Display Resolution and DVGA Explained

When Apple introduced the iPhone 4 in 2010, it debuted the "Retina display," setting a new benchmark for mobile screen clarity. This article examines the technical specifications of the iPhone 4 display, explains its 960-by-640 resolution, and details how this configuration directly corresponds to the display standard known as Double Video Graphics Array (DVGA).

The iPhone 4 Retina Display Resolution

The iPhone 4 featured a 3.5-inch screen with a native resolution of 960 × 640 pixels. This yielded a pixel density of 326 pixels per inch (ppi). Apple branded the panel as a "Retina display" because, at a typical viewing distance of 10 to 12 inches, the human eye cannot distinguish individual pixels at densities exceeding roughly 300 ppi. The result was sharp text, smooth curves, and photographic detail that eliminated visible pixelation.

How It Relates to Double Video Graphics Array (DVGA)

To understand how the iPhone 4 relates to DVGA, it helps to examine standard display acronyms:

  • VGA (Video Graphics Array): Standard VGA has a resolution of 640 × 480 pixels in a 4:3 aspect ratio.
  • HVGA (Half VGA): A resolution of 480 × 320 pixels represents exactly half the pixels of standard VGA, configured in a 3:2 aspect ratio. Apple used HVGA in the original iPhone, iPhone 3G, and iPhone 3GS.
  • DVGA (Double VGA): DVGA denotes a resolution of 960 × 640 pixels.

The iPhone 4 screen is a direct implementation of DVGA. When designing the Retina display, Apple doubled the number of pixels across both dimensions of the previous 480 × 320 (HVGA) displays:

  • \(480 \times 2 = 960\text{ pixels}\)
  • \(320 \times 2 = 640\text{ pixels}\)

This scaling quadrupled the overall pixel count from 153,600 pixels to 614,400 pixels while maintaining the identical 3:2 aspect ratio and physical 3.5-inch screen size. Because it doubled both the vertical and horizontal pixel lines of the earlier HVGA standard, the 960 × 640 specification became widely categorized in computing hardware terminology as Double Video Graphics Array (DVGA).

This 2x scale factor allowed Apple to achieve extreme visual sharpness while maintaining backward compatibility for software, as legacy iOS applications could simply scale their graphic assets by a clean factor of two without distortion.
