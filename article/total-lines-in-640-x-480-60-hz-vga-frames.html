Total Lines in 640x480 60Hz VGA Frames

In a standard 640x480 60Hz Video Graphics Array (VGA) signal, a total of 525 lines are transmitted per frame. While only 480 of these lines contain visible video data, the remaining 45 lines form the vertical blanking interval. This article breaks down the exact timing components of the 640x480 60Hz standard and details how vertical blanking accounts for the total line count.

Timing Breakdown of the 525 Lines

The vertical frame structure of standard VGA timing is divided into active display lines and three distinct vertical blanking stages:

  • Active Display (480 lines): These are the visible horizontal scan lines containing active picture information rendered on the display.
  • Vertical Front Porch (10 lines): A short delay of blank lines transmitted immediately after the active display concludes, ensuring the display controller has completed the frame data.
  • Vertical Sync Pulse (2 lines): A synchronization signal that instructs the display hardware to reset its scan beam to the top of the screen.
  • Vertical Back Porch (33 lines): A stabilization delay allowing the display circuitry and electron gun (in cathode-ray tubes) to settle before rendering active pixels for the next frame.

Adding these components together yields the total frame line count:

\[\text{Total Lines} = 480 + 10 + 2 + 33 = 525 \text{ lines}\]

Relationship to Horizontal Timing and Clock Speed

The total frame line count corresponds directly to the standard VGA pixel clock:

  • Horizontal Line Total: Each line contains 800 pixel clocks (640 active pixels + 16 front porch + 96 sync pulse + 48 back porch).
  • Frame Pixel Total: 800 horizontal pixels \(\times\) 525 vertical lines = 420,000 total pixel clocks per frame.
  • Clock Frequency: Standard VGA operates at a nominal pixel clock of 25.175 MHz.

Dividing the 25.175 MHz clock by 420,000 total pixels per frame yields a vertical refresh rate of approximately 59.94 Hz, commonly referenced as 60Hz.
