1920x1080 VGA Resolution Naming Standard

This article explains the naming standard for a 1920x1080 resolution signal transmitted across an analog Video Graphics Array (VGA) connection. While early computer display modes used specific "...XGA" extensions for different resolutions, the industry transitioned to broadcast terminology for widescreen 16:9 formats. The sections below clarify the official standard designation, the technical mechanics of transmitting this resolution over analog cabling, and where it fits within legacy display hierarchy.

The Standard Name: Full HD (FHD)

The standardized name for a 1920x1080 display resolution is Full HD or FHD (commonly referenced as 1080p).

Unlike traditional computer resolutions derived from the IBM PC lineage—such as XGA (1024x768), SXGA (1280x1024), and UXGA (1600x1200)—the 1920x1080 resolution originated from digital television formats rather than the VESA computer graphics naming scheme. In computer standards:

  • WUXGA (Widescreen Ultra Extended Graphics Array) refers strictly to 1920x1200, which uses a 16:10 aspect ratio.
  • FHD (Full High Definition) refers to 1920x1080, which uses a 16:9 aspect ratio.

When transmitted over an analog VGA connection, 1920x1080 is formally categorized under VESA Display Monitor Timing (DMT) and Coordinated Video Timings (CVT) as 1080p or FHD.

Technical Operation Over Analog VGA

VGA is inherently an analog interface that transmits red, green, and blue (RGB) color channels alongside horizontal and vertical synchronization signals. Because it is an analog medium, VGA has no hardcoded digital limit on resolution, provided the following hardware requirements are met:

  • Pixel Clock Bandwidth: Transmitting 1920x1080 at a standard 60 Hz refresh rate requires a pixel clock of approximately 148.5 MHz (or roughly 138.5 MHz using reduced blanking). Both the graphics adapter's RAMDAC (Random Access Memory Digital-to-Analog Converter) and the display's internal ADC (Analog-to-Digital Converter) must support this frequency.
  • Cable Shielding: High pixel clock rates over analog wiring are susceptible to crosstalk, ghosting, and signal degradation. Proper 75-ohm coaxial shielding for the RGB lines inside the VGA cable is required to maintain a sharp FHD image.

Despite being designed originally for 640x480 resolution in 1987, the DE-15 analog connector reliably carries 1920x1080 FHD signals on compatible modern equipment.
