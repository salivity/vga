Total Scan Lines in a Standard 640x480 VGA Signal

A standard 640x480 Video Graphics Array (VGA) signal operating at 60 Hz contains exactly 525 total horizontal scan lines per frame when including the vertical blanking interval. While only 480 of these lines are utilized to render active picture data on the screen, the remaining 45 lines are dedicated to the vertical synchronization process, ensuring legacy cathode-ray tube (CRT) monitors and modern digital controllers properly align and frame the incoming image.

Vertical Timing Breakdown

The vertical timing of the industry-standard 640x480 @ 60 Hz timing specification (originally established by IBM and standardized by VESA) is partitioned into four distinct phases:

  • Active Display: 480 lines (the visible image)
  • Vertical Front Porch: 10 lines
  • Vertical Sync Pulse: 2 lines
  • Vertical Back Porch: 33 lines

Adding these intervals together results in the total frame height:

\[\text{Total Vertical Lines} = 480 + 10 + 2 + 33 = 525\text{ lines}\]

The Role of Blanking Lines

The 45 non-visible lines constitute the vertical blanking interval (VBI). During this period, the video signal is pulled down to a blanking level so the electron beam in a traditional CRT monitor can travel from the bottom-right corner of the screen back up to the top-left corner (vertical retrace) without drawing stray lines across the visible display area.

  • The front porch provides a short delay after the final visible line before triggering the sync signal, preventing premature synchronization.
  • The sync pulse commands the display hardware to initiate the vertical retrace.
  • The back porch provides settling time after the sync pulse to stabilize the deflection circuitry before the next frame's active video lines begin.

Impact on Overall Signal Frequency

The 525 total lines directly determine the scan rates of a standard VGA interface. With a frame refresh rate of approximately 59.94 Hz (often rounded to 60 Hz), the horizontal line frequency is calculated by multiplying the total lines by the refresh rate:

\[525\text{ lines} \times 59.94\text{ Hz} \approx 31.469\text{ kHz}\]

A total of 525 horizontal lines scanned 59.94 times per second yields the classic 31.469 kHz horizontal scan rate, supported by a 25.175 MHz pixel clock to accommodate 800 total horizontal pixel clocks per line.
