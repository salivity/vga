Function of Pin 5 on a Standard VGA Connector

This article provides an overview of pin 5 on a standard 15-pin Video Graphics Array (VGA) connector, detailing its technical role in video transmission. You will learn about its primary assignment as a ground connection, how modern graphics adapters utilize it for cable detection, and its integration within VESA display standards.

Primary Role: Digital Ground

On a standard 15-pin DE-15 VGA connector, pin 5 is designated as a ground connection, specifically referred to as Digital Ground or Ground (GND). While pins 6, 7, and 8 serve as analog grounds dedicated to shielding the individual Red, Green, and Blue color channels from interference, pin 5 provides a return path for digital logic and synchronization circuitry.

Cable Detection and Monitor Self-Test

Beyond acting as a passive ground, pin 5 plays a crucial role in hardware detection:

  • Connection Detection: Many graphics cards use pin 5 to detect whether a display cable is physically plugged into the port. By monitoring the electrical state of pin 5, the graphics controller can identify if a display is present.
  • Self-Test Loop: In several monitor designs, pin 5 is tied directly to ground internally. When a computer sends a low-voltage test signal through this pin, the grounded return allows the system—or the monitor itself—to run a basic self-test routine to confirm that a valid connection exists before initiating full signal transmission.

Integration with VESA DDC

Under the Video Electronics Standards Association (VESA) Display Data Channel (DDC) specifications, pin 5 functions as the DDC Ground (or DDC Return). The DDC protocol allows the monitor to send its Extended Display Identification Data (EDID)—such as supported resolutions, refresh rates, and color profiles—back to the host computer using pins 12 (SDA) and 15 (SCL). Pin 5 supplies the necessary reference ground for this I²C-based digital communication, ensuring reliable data exchange between the graphics card and the monitor.
