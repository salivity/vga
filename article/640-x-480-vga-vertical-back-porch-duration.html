640x480 VGA Vertical Back Porch Duration

In standard 640x480 Video Graphics Array (VGA) timing at a 60 Hz refresh rate, the vertical back porch has a duration of 33 scan lines, which translates to approximately 1.049 milliseconds (1,048.66 microseconds). This period occurs immediately after the vertical synchronization pulse and directly before the active display lines begin, giving the display hardware sufficient time to prepare for rendering the next frame.

Standard 640x480 @ 60 Hz Vertical Specifications

The standard industry timing established by VESA for 640x480 at 60 Hz operates on a full frame of 525 vertical scan lines:

  • Active Video Area: 480 lines (15.253 ms)
  • Vertical Front Porch: 10 lines (0.318 ms)
  • Vertical Sync Pulse: 2 lines (0.064 ms)
  • Vertical Back Porch: 33 lines (1.049 ms)
  • Total Frame: 525 lines (16.683 ms / ~59.94 Hz)

Calculation of Time Duration

The exact time duration of the vertical back porch is derived from the standard pixel clock and horizontal line timing:

  1. Pixel Clock Frequency: 25.175 MHz
  2. Total Horizontal Line Duration: 800 pixel clocks \[\text{Line Duration} = \frac{800}{25,175,000\text{ Hz}} \approx 31.7776\ \mu\text{s}\]
  3. Horizontal Line Frequency: \[\frac{1}{31.7776\ \mu\text{s}} \approx 31.469\text{ kHz}\]
  4. Vertical Back Porch Time: \[33\text{ lines} \times 31.7776\ \mu\text{s} \approx 1,048.66\ \mu\text{s}\ (1.04866\text{ ms})\]

Purpose of the Vertical Back Porch

In cathode-ray tube (CRT) monitors, the vertical back porch provided the physical time necessary for the electron beam to reset from the bottom of the screen to the top (vertical retrace) and stabilize before scanning the first active line of video. In modern digital displays and FPGA video controllers, this blanking period remains mandatory for maintaining standard signal synchronization and compatibility across display interfaces.
