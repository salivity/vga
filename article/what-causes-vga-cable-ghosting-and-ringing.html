What Causes VGA Cable Ghosting and Ringing?

Ghosting and ringing artifacts in long, unshielded Video Graphics Array (VGA) cables are primarily caused by impedance mismatches, signal reflections, cable capacitance, and external electromagnetic interference. Because VGA relies on high-frequency analog signals to transmit red, green, and blue video data, any physical degradation of the electrical waveform translates directly into visible visual flaws on the display.

Impedance Mismatches and Signal Reflections

The primary cause of both ghosting and ringing is impedance mismatching. Standard VGA transmission lines and termination loads are designed for a characteristic impedance of 75 ohms. When an unshielded or poorly constructed cable deviates from this 75-ohm standard, the high-frequency video signal is not entirely absorbed by the receiving display.

Instead, a portion of the electrical energy reflects backward down the cable toward the source and bounces back again toward the monitor. Because electrical signals travel at a finite speed, this reflected wave arrives at the display slightly later than the original signal. The delayed signal is drawn slightly to the right of the intended pixels, creating a duplicate, faint trace known as "ghosting." If the reflected wave bounces repeatedly, it causes rapid voltage oscillations across sharp color transitions, producing the multi-line ripples known as "ringing."

Cable Capacitance and Signal Attenuation

Long cables inherently introduce electrical resistance and parasitic capacitance. In an analog video signal, sharp transitions between light and dark pixels require high-frequency waveforms with rapid rise and fall times.

As cable length increases, parasitic capacitance between the conductors acts as a low-pass filter, dissipating high frequencies to ground. This rounds off the square edges of the signal pulses. When the signal cannot change voltage fast enough to represent distinct pixel boundaries, high-contrast borders smear across the screen. This attenuation also alters the dynamic impedance of the line, worsening the conditions that cause ringing.

Lack of Individual Shielding and Crosstalk

VGA cables carry five primary signals: red, green, blue, horizontal sync, and vertical sync. In high-quality VGA cables, each of the three color lines is an individually shielded, 75-ohm coaxial sub-cable.

Unshielded cables bundle these lines together without individual isolation. Without internal shielding, the rapid voltage swings in one color line induce phantom voltages in adjacent conductors through capacitive and inductive coupling, a phenomenon known as crosstalk. This causes the color information from one channel to bleed into another, resulting in tinted halos around objects.

Electromagnetic and Radio Frequency Interference (EMI/RFI)

Unshielded cables act as antennas. Over long distances, they absorb ambient electromagnetic noise emitted by nearby power supplies, fluorescent lights, and other electronic devices. This external interference disrupts the analog voltage levels, adding high-frequency static, edge jitter, and wavy lines to the image that intensify the appearance of ghosting and ringing artifacts.
