Total Pixels in a 640x480 VGA Screen

A standard 640x480 Video Graphics Array (VGA) screen contains exactly 307,200 pixels. This article provides a clear breakdown of how that pixel count is calculated, details the specifications associated with the 640x480 display format, and explains the historical context of the VGA standard in computing.

The Pixel Calculation

To find the total number of pixels on any digital display, you multiply the horizontal resolution by the vertical resolution.

  • Horizontal Pixels (Width): 640
  • Vertical Pixels (Height): 480

\[\text{Total Pixels} = 640 \times 480 = 307,200\text{ pixels}\]

In modern digital imaging terms, 307,200 pixels equals approximately 0.3 megapixels (MP).

Display Specifications of 640x480 VGA

The 640x480 resolution adheres to specific parameters that defined computing visuals for decades:

  • Aspect Ratio: The ratio of width to height is 4:3. When you simplify the fraction 640/480 by dividing both numbers by their greatest common divisor (160), you get the standard full-screen CRT aspect ratio of 4:3.
  • Scan Method: Original VGA displays used progressive or interlaced CRT scanning, commonly running at a 60 Hz refresh rate.
  • Color Depth: The baseline IBM VGA standard supported a 16-color palette at 640x480 resolution from a master palette of 262,144 colors (6 bits per channel for RGB). Later hardware iterations expanded this to 256 colors (8-bit) and True Color (24-bit).

Background and Relevance of VGA

Introduced by IBM in 1987 for the PS/2 line of computers, Video Graphics Array quickly became the baseline hardware standard for the PC industry.

While modern monitors frequently run at 1920x1080 (Full HD, with over 2 million pixels) or 3840x2160 (4K UHD, with over 8.2 million pixels), 640x480 remains a critical legacy benchmark. Because the base VGA specifications are hardcoded into display controllers and BIOS firmware, systems often revert to 640x480 as a failsafe mode when advanced graphics drivers crash or during basic system recovery operations.
