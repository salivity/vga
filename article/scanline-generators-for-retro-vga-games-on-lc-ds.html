Scanline Generators for Retro VGA Games on LCDs

A scanline generator plays a crucial role in modern retro gaming setups by recreating the visual characteristics of vintage cathode-ray tube (CRT) monitors on modern liquid crystal displays (LCDs). Video Graphics Array (VGA) games from the late 1980s and 1990s were designed around the physical properties of CRTs, which naturally softened raw pixels and added horizontal blanking lines. On modern flat panels, these low-resolution signals appear unnaturally sharp, blocky, and washed out. A scanline generator bridges this technological gap by physically or digitally inserting horizontal blank lines into the image, restoring the intended aesthetic, contrast, and depth of classic VGA titles.

The Display Disconnect Between CRTs and LCDs

During the golden era of PC and arcade gaming, standard VGA output operated primarily at resolutions such as 320x200, 320x240, and 640x480. CRT monitors drew these images line by line using an electron beam. In lower resolution modes, the beam skipped physical space between each active horizontal sweep, creating visible, dark gaps known as scanlines. Game artists relied heavily on these gaps and phosphor bloom to blend dithered colors, smooth jagged edges, and create an illusion of higher detail.

Modern LCDs function fundamentally differently. Instead of an analog beam, LCDs utilize a fixed matrix of physical pixels. When fed a low-resolution VGA signal, an LCD scales the image up to fit its native resolution—such as 1080p or 4K. This process renders every individual source pixel as a harsh, uniform square. Without the natural blending of a CRT, art assets lose their intended coherence, dithering looks like static noise, and colors often appear flat.

How a Scanline Generator Operates

A scanline generator is typically an inline hardware device connected directly to the analog VGA cable between the gaming hardware and the display, though software shaders can emulate the process. In hardware implementations, the device intercepts the RGB color signals and sync lines.

When active, the generator blanks out or dims alternating horizontal lines of the video feed:

  • Signal Interception: The device monitors the horizontal synchronization (H-Sync) signal to track which line of the image is currently being drawn.
  • Line Blanking: On alternating lines (typically odd or even lines, depending on the configuration), the generator grounds or heavily attenuates the red, green, and blue analog color lines.
  • Real-Time Output: The result is a consistent pattern of dark horizontal lines running through the scaled image, delivered instantly to the monitor without digital frame buffering.

Performance and Visual Benefits

Using a dedicated scanline generator offers several practical advantages for retro computing and arcade enthusiasts:

  • Zero Input Latency: Hardware scanline generators modify the analog signal in real time using passive or low-power active circuitry. Unlike complex digital post-processing filters, they do not store frames in memory, ensuring that gameplay remains responsive.
  • Perceived Sharpness and Contrast: By reintroducing black gaps between pixels, the human eye perceives increased contrast. The dark gaps break up the large, solid blocks of color that make up upscaled 240p or 480p imagery.
  • Artifact Masking: Vintage games frequently used checkerboard dithering patterns to simulate transparency or extended color palettes. Scanlines mask these patterns, causing the eye to blend the colors naturally rather than perceiving distinct dots.
  • Customization: Most hardware generators feature switches to adjust line width, choose between odd and even lines, or utilize potentiometers to control the darkness of the scanlines, allowing players to match the look to different eras of CRT monitors.

By reintroducing the native display properties that game artists originally designed for, a scanline generator transforms flat, clinical LCD presentations into authentic, visually balanced retro gaming experiences.
