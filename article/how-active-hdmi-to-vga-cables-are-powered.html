How Active HDMI to VGA Cables Are Powered

Active HDMI to VGA cables require electrical power to operate an integrated digital-to-analog converter (DAC), which transforms digital bitstreams into analog video signals. Under standard operating conditions, these cables draw their operating power directly from the host HDMI port via a dedicated 5-volt power pin. However, depending on the host hardware's power delivery and the energy demands of the conversion chipset, supplemental power may occasionally be sourced through an auxiliary connection.

The Primary Power Source: HDMI Pin 18

The primary power source for an HDMI to VGA cable is the host device itself—such as a computer, media player, or game console. The standard HDMI specification includes a dedicated power rail on Pin 18, designated as the "+5V Power" line.

When connected, the source device transmits a continuous 5-volt direct current (DC) across this pin alongside the ground connection on Pin 17. The low-power DAC microchip inside the cable's connector housing taps directly into this 5V DC line to energize its internal logic, decode the Transition-Minimized Differential Signaling (TMDS) digital data, and output the corresponding analog RGB and sync signals required by VGA displays.

Power Constraints of the HDMI Specification

While Pin 18 provides native power, the HDMI standard only mandates that source devices supply a minimum current of 55 milliamperes (mA). Most modern conversion chipsets require between 150 mA and 300 mA to process high-resolution video streams (such as 1080p at 60Hz).

While desktop graphics cards and powered motherboards typically provide well over the 55 mA minimum, low-power devices—such as ultrabooks, tablets, streaming sticks, and certain gaming consoles—often adhere strictly to the baseline limit or limit current output to save battery. When an active cable attempts to draw more current than the host port can supply, the connection may suffer from flickering, signal degradation, or a complete failure to display an image.

Auxiliary Power via USB

To overcome scenarios where the host HDMI port provides insufficient current, many active HDMI to VGA adapters include an integrated secondary power port, typically a micro-USB or USB-C connector.

When connected to an external USB power supply or an adjacent USB port on the host machine, this auxiliary line bypasses Pin 18 to deliver a stable, dedicated 5-volt feed to the converter chip. This external power ensures the active chipset operates continuously without overdrawing current from the host's HDMI bus.
