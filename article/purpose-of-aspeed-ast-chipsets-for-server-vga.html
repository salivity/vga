Purpose of ASPEED AST Chipsets for Server VGA

This article explains the function and significance of ASPEED AST-series chipsets in server environments, focusing on their role in delivering Video Graphics Array (VGA) output. You will learn how these specialized chips combine Baseboard Management Controller (BMC) capabilities with integrated 2D graphics to provide essential display output, enable remote management via KVM-over-IP, and maintain low-power visual diagnostic capabilities for enterprise hardware.

Dedicated Baseboard Management Controller (BMC) Integration

ASPEED AST-series chips (such as the AST2400, AST2500, and AST2600) are not standalone graphics cards. Instead, they are system-on-chip (SoC) Baseboard Management Controllers that integrate an embedded 2D graphics processor. Their primary purpose is out-of-band server management, with video output serving as an interface to monitor and configure the physical machine.

Local Display for Datacenter "Crash Carts"

Enterprise servers typically lack consumer-grade GPUs or integrated display engines on their primary CPUs. The ASPEED AST chipset provides a basic, reliable physical VGA port on the front or rear of the server chassis. This interface enables datacenter technicians to connect a local monitor, keyboard, and mouse—commonly referred to as a "crash cart" or rackmount console—to troubleshoot boot failures, configure BIOS/UEFI firmware, or manage network settings locally without relying on the operating system.

Hardware-Level Video Redirection (KVM-over-IP)

A critical role of the ASPEED AST graphics core is capturing display data for remote administration. The chipset features a built-in video capture and compression engine. It takes the digital video stream generated by the server's console, compresses it into lightweight formats (such as JPEG or H.264), and transmits it over the management network via IPMI, Redfish, or a web-based interface. This allows administrators worldwide to view the server's desktop or command line interface as if they were standing in front of the physical machine.

Independence from the Host System

The graphics controller in an ASPEED AST chipset operates independently of the server's main CPUs and operating system. Powered by an auxiliary power rail, it remains functional even when the main host system is powered down, rebooting, or experiencing an OS-level crash (such as a kernel panic or Blue Screen of Death). This continuous operation ensures administrators can view pre-boot sequences, POST codes, and critical error messages that standard GPUs cannot display during system failures.

Low Power and Legacy Standards

Server environments prioritize energy efficiency, space savings, and long-term reliability over high resolution or 3D acceleration. The ASPEED AST series delivers sufficient resolution (typically up to 1920x1200) to navigate management consoles while consuming negligible power. The use of the legacy VGA standard guarantees broad compatibility with existing datacenter rack infrastructure, KVM switches, and multi-port console switches without requiring specialized digital display converters.
