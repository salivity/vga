VGA 640x480 Vertical Front Porch Timing

In standard 640x480 Video Graphics Array (VGA) timing at a 60 Hz refresh rate, the vertical front porch has a duration of exactly 10 horizontal lines, which equates to approximately 0.318 milliseconds (317.78 microseconds). This article details the specific duration, timing specifications, and mathematical calculations defining the vertical front porch within the standard industry-defined 640x480 VGA signal framework.

Standard Duration Specifications

Under the standard established by the Video Electronics Standards Association (VESA) for 640x480 at 60 Hz:

  • Duration in Lines: 10 lines
  • Duration in Time: ~317.78 µs (0.318 ms)
  • Duration in Pixel Clocks: 8,000 clock cycles

Full Vertical Frame Context

A complete VGA frame consists of 525 total vertical scan lines. The vertical cycle is divided into four distinct regions:

  1. Active Video: 480 lines (the visible display area)
  2. Vertical Front Porch: 10 lines (blanking period before synchronization)
  3. Vertical Sync Pulse: 2 lines (negative polarity pulse signalling the start of a new frame)
  4. Vertical Back Porch: 33 lines (blanking period allowing electron beam or digital receiver stabilization)

Calculating the Time Duration

The exact duration is derived from the standard VGA pixel clock frequency and line timing parameters:

  1. Pixel Clock Frequency (\(f_{pixel}\)): 25.175 MHz (approximately 39.722 nanoseconds per pixel).
  2. Horizontal Line Duration (\(T_{line}\)): A full horizontal line consists of 800 pixel clocks (640 visible pixels + 16 front porch + 96 sync pulse + 48 back porch).
    \[T_{line} = \frac{800 \text{ pixels}}{25.175 \text{ MHz}} \approx 31.7775 \text{ microseconds}\]
  3. Front Porch Calculation: Multiplying the horizontal scan duration by the 10 front porch lines yields the total duration:
    \[\text{Duration} = 10 \times 31.7775 \mu\text{s} \approx 317.78 \mu\text{s}\]

During this 10-line interval, the Red, Green, and Blue analog lines are held at blanking level (0V or black), ensuring no beam trace or digital artifacts appear as the signal transitions from the active display area to the vertical synchronization pulse.
