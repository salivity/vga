What Voltage Range Is Used for VGA RGB Color Signals?

Video Graphics Array (VGA) interfaces carry analog video via three dedicated color lines: red, green, and blue. In standard implementations, these color signals operate within a peak-to-peak voltage range of 0.0 V to 0.7 V into a standard 75-ohm termination load. A voltage of 0.0 V represents full black (blanking level), while 0.7 V corresponds to maximum color saturation. Understanding this voltage specification is essential for video hardware design, analog-to-digital conversion, and troubleshooting display signal degradation.

Standard Signal Levels and Impedance

The VGA electrical specification defines an analog transmission line designed to drive a 75-ohm load at the display end, matched by a 75-ohm source impedance at the graphics card output:

  • Black Level (Zero Intensity): 0.0 V
  • Maximum Intensity (Full Saturation): 0.7 V
  • Peak-to-Peak Amplitude: 0.7 Vp-p
  • Nominal Impedance: 75 ohms

When no active display data is being transmitted during the horizontal and vertical blanking intervals, the RGB lines remain held at 0.0 V. Any voltage measured significantly above 0.7 V under a proper load typically indicates an impedance mismatch or an incorrectly configured DAC (Digital-to-Analog Converter).

Unloaded vs. Terminated Measurements

Because the transmission circuit forms a voltage divider between the source impedance and the monitor load, termination directly impacts measured voltages:

  • Terminated (Connected to Monitor): When connected to a standard display terminating each RGB line with a 75-ohm resistor to ground, the voltage ranges from 0.0 V to 0.7 V.
  • Unloaded (Open Circuit): If measured with an oscilloscope or high-impedance multimeter without a 75-ohm termination load in place, the output voltages float to roughly double the nominal values, showing a swing from 0.0 V to approximately 1.4 V.

Synchronization Signal Differences

A common point of confusion involves the synchronization signals running alongside the RGB color lines. While the red, green, and blue lines carry high-frequency analog signals capped at 0.7 V, the horizontal sync (HSYNC) and vertical sync (VSYNC) lines operate under standard digital logic levels. HSYNC and VSYNC typically utilize 5.0 V or 3.3 V TTL (Transistor-Transistor Logic) square waves rather than low-voltage analog signals.

Signal Integrity and Color Accuracy

Because the color data is purely analog, even millivolt variations in the 0.0 V to 0.7 V window directly affect color rendition. Voltage drop over long cable runs, poor cable shielding, or resistance drift can compress the dynamic range, resulting in dimmer images, washed-out whites, or color tint shifts across the screen. Maintaining strict adherence to the 0.7 V standard ensures accurate gamma and color reproduction across all VGA-compliant displays.
