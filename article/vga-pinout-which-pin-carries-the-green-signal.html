VGA Pinout: Which Pin Carries the Green Signal?

On a standard DE-15 Video Graphics Array (VGA) connector, pin 2 is dedicated to transmitting the green analog video signal. This guide outlines the exact pin location, the role of its associated ground return line, and the diagnostic implications when this specific pin or wire fails.

The Green Video Line: Pin 2

The standard DE-15 VGA connector uses a three-row, 15-pin layout to deliver analog component video signals (RGBHV). Pin 2 is situated in the top row, between Pin 1 (Red Video) and Pin 3 (Blue Video). It transmits the analog green color information at a nominal voltage level of 0.7 volts peak-to-peak into a standard 75-ohm impedance load.

The Associated Ground: Pin 7

High-frequency analog video requires proper shielding to prevent signal degradation and electromagnetic interference. Pin 2 is paired with Pin 7, located directly below it in the middle row of the connector. Pin 7 functions as the dedicated ground return line for the green video channel. Without a reliable connection on Pin 7, the green color channel can suffer from ghosting, ringing, or video noise.

Primary RGB Pinout Overview

For context, the analog color pins and their matching grounds occupy the first two rows of the DE-15 interface:

  • Pin 1: Red Video (Analog)
  • Pin 2: Green Video (Analog)
  • Pin 3: Blue Video (Analog)
  • Pin 6: Red Ground Return
  • Pin 7: Green Ground Return
  • Pin 8: Blue Ground Return

Troubleshooting Pin 2 Failures

When Pin 2 is bent, recessed, or disconnected due to a broken internal wire, the display will lose its entire green color spectrum. Because visual color is created through additive RGB color mixing, the loss of green leaves only red and blue active, causing the entire image to display with a distinct purple or magenta tint.
