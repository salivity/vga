Oscilloscope Waveform of an Active VGA Channel

An active analog Video Graphics Array (VGA) color channel produces an oscilloscope waveform characterized by high-frequency voltage fluctuations bounded between 0.0 volts and 0.7 volts peak-to-peak across periodic horizontal line intervals. When probed against a standard 75-ohm termination, the signal alternates between flat zero-volt blanking intervals and dynamic video payloads that correlate directly with on-screen pixel intensity.

Voltage Levels and Signal Amplitudes

A properly terminated analog VGA color line (Red, Green, or Blue) operates strictly within a 0.7 Vpp window:

  • 0.0 Volts (Baseline/Blanking): Represents complete darkness or the blanking level.
  • 0.7 Volts (Peak): Represents maximum color saturation.
  • Intermediate Voltages: Represent proportional shades of the specific color channel.

If the channel is measured without proper 75-ohm termination (such as with a high-impedance 1 MΩ probe on an unterminated cable), the amplitude will double to roughly 1.4 Vpp.

Horizontal Timebase Characteristics

At a timebase aligned with the line rate (typically tens of microseconds, such as 5 µs/div to 10 µs/div):

  1. Active Video Region: Occupies the majority of the horizontal period. For complex graphical displays, this area appears as a dense, rapidly varying, irregular envelope of high-frequency pulses shifting between 0.0V and 0.7V. If displaying a standard test pattern like color bars, the waveform forms a crisp, discrete staircase pattern descending or ascending in distinct voltage steps.
  2. Horizontal Blanking Interval: A flat, clean line held strictly at 0.0V. This interval encompasses the front porch, the sync pulse duration, and the back porch. Unlike composite video (which includes negative-going sync pulses dropping to -0.3V), separate-sync VGA carries synchronization signals on distinct H-Sync and V-Sync pins, leaving the color channels purely at 0.0V during line resets.

Vertical Timebase Characteristics

When the oscilloscope timebase is adjusted to the millisecond scale (e.g., 2 ms/div to 5 ms/div) to capture entire frames:

  • Field Envelope: The waveform appears as a solid block of modulated high-frequency signals corresponding to the active display lines of the frame.
  • Vertical Blanking Interval (VBI): A prolonged, completely flat 0.0V baseline appearing periodically at the vertical refresh rate (e.g., every 16.6 ms for a standard 60 Hz mode). During this interval, the electron beam or raster counter resets to the top of the frame, suppressing all color output.
