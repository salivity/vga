CRT Emudriver: Low Pixel Clocks for Retro Arcade CRTs

This article examines CRT Emudriver, a specialized graphics driver suite designed to output native low-frequency video signals from standard PC hardware to vintage displays. It covers the fundamental hardware conflict between standard PC video output and legacy cathode-ray tube (CRT) monitors, explains how CRT Emudriver overrides modern GPU limitations to unlock ultra-low horizontal refresh rates, and details the manipulation of Video Graphics Array (VGA) pixel clocks and the implementation of super resolutions to achieve authentic, cycle-accurate arcade visuals.

The Conflict Between Modern PCs and Classic CRTs

Standard personal computers and modern graphics cards are engineered around standard Video Graphics Array (VGA) specifications and digital display standards. Modern analog VGA outputs operate at a minimum horizontal scan frequency of 31.5 kHz, which corresponds to a progressive resolution of 640x480 at 60 Hz.

In contrast, vintage arcade cabinets and consumer CRT televisions rely on standard-resolution displays that expect a horizontal scan frequency of roughly 15.7 kHz. These displays draw progressive images at lower resolutions—most commonly 240p (around 320x240) at roughly 60 Hz. Connecting an unmodified PC outputting a 31 kHz signal directly to a 15 kHz arcade chassis can result in an unreadable image and potentially cause permanent electrical damage to the CRT's flyback transformer or deflection circuitry.

What Is CRT Emudriver?

Developed primarily by programmer Calamity, CRT Emudriver is a customized, patched graphics driver for AMD/ATI Radeon graphics cards running on Windows. Its primary function is to remove hardware-level limitations that prevent modern GPUs from generating legacy video timings.

CRT Emudriver unlocks the ability to define custom Modelines—detailed timing profiles that dictate horizontal front porches, sync pulses, back porches, and vertical timings. Paired with companion utilities like VMMaker and Switchres (often integrated with GroovyMAME), CRT Emudriver dynamically creates and switches video modes to match the exact resolution and refresh rate of original arcade hardware, such as a Neo Geo running at 320x224 at 59.18 Hz or a Capcom CPS-2 board running at 384x224 at 59.62 Hz.

Manipulating the Pixel Clock (Dot Clock)

The pixel clock (or dot clock) defines the rate at which pixels are transmitted from the GPU’s digital-to-analog converter (RAMDAC) to the display. Mathematically, the pixel clock is the product of the total horizontal pixel count (visible pixels plus blanking intervals), the total vertical line count, and the vertical refresh rate:

\[\text{Pixel Clock} = \text{Total Horizontal Pixels} \times \text{Total Vertical Lines} \times \text{Refresh Rate}\]

For a standard 15 kHz arcade resolution (e.g., 320x240 at 60 Hz), the total pixel count per frame is relatively low, requiring a pixel clock between 6 MHz and 8 MHz.

Modern GPUs present two core obstacles to achieving these frequencies:

  1. Driver Locks: Stock AMD/NVIDIA drivers enforce minimum resolution and pixel clock safety thresholds (often refusing anything below 25 MHz to adhere to VESA standards).
  2. Phase-Locked Loop (PLL) Hardware Limits: The GPU’s clock generators (PLLs) can only divide clock signals down to a certain physical threshold. Many Radeon cards cannot reliably generate a stable analog clock signal below 10 MHz or 12 MHz without signal jitter or total clock failure.

CRT Emudriver addresses the first barrier by modifying the driver’s kernel-level parameters, allowing direct access to the CRTC (Cathode Ray Tube Controller) registers of supported Radeon GPUs. This enables the card to output 15.7 kHz horizontal synchronization pulses natively via the analog pins (RGBHV) of a standard DVI-I or VGA port.

Overcoming Hardware Limits with "Super Resolutions"

When a target retro resolution requires a pixel clock lower than the GPU's hardware PLL can physically output, CRT Emudriver and Switchres resolve the limitation through the use of horizontal "super resolutions."

Because CRT monitors are analog devices, they are structurally constrained only by vertical scanlines and horizontal deflection frequency (15 kHz); they do not possess a fixed horizontal grid of digital pixels. The electron beam sweeps continuously across a horizontal line.

CRT Emudriver exploits this characteristic by scaling only the horizontal axis to a large, fixed value (typically 2560 pixels) while leaving the vertical scanline count and refresh rate at their native target (e.g., 2560x240 at 60 Hz):

  • Vertical Axis: Retains the exact native line count (240 lines). The CRT draws each scanline at the correct physical thickness and spacing, preserving native scanlines.
  • Horizontal Axis: Expanding 320 pixels to 2560 pixels multiplies the required data throughput by a factor of eight.
  • Pixel Clock Adjustment: A resolution of 2560x240 at 60 Hz pushes the required dot clock from ~6.4 MHz up to ~51.2 MHz.

A 51.2 MHz clock falls comfortably within the optimal operating range of modern GPU PLLs. The CRT’s analog phosphors and low-pass filtering seamlessly integrate the ultra-dense horizontal points along the scanline, resulting in a display that is mathematically identical in timing, geometry, and appearance to the original arcade source without introducing vertical scaling artifacts or motion judder.
