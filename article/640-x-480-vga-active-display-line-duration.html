640x480 VGA Active Display Line Duration

This article provides an analysis of the timing specifications for a standard 640x480 Video Graphics Array (VGA) signal operating at 60 Hz. It details the exact calculation used to determine the active display duration of a single horizontal scanline in microseconds, breaking down the pixel clock frequency and horizontal timing parameters.

The duration of the active display portion in a standard 640x480 VGA line is approximately 25.42 microseconds (\(\mu\)s).

Calculation Breakdown

Standard industry specifications define a 640x480 display at a 60 Hz refresh rate using the following parameters:

  • Active Pixels per Line: 640 pixels
  • Pixel Clock Frequency: 25.175 MHz (\(25,175,000 \text{ Hz}\))

To calculate the time required to draw the active pixel portion of a single line:

  1. Determine the duration of a single pixel clock cycle: \[\text{Pixel Duration} = \frac{1}{25,175,000 \text{ Hz}} \approx 39.722 \text{ nanoseconds (ns)}\]

  2. Multiply the single-pixel duration by the 640 active horizontal pixels: \[\text{Active Display Duration} = 640 \times 39.722 \text{ ns} \approx 25,422.05 \text{ ns}\]

  3. Convert nanoseconds to microseconds: \[25,422.05 \text{ ns} \approx 25.422 \text{ }\mu\text{s}\]

Full Horizontal Line Context

A complete horizontal line includes both the visible display time and the horizontal blanking interval:

  • Active Video: 640 pixels (\(25.42 \text{ }\mu\text{s}\))
  • Front Porch: 16 pixels (\(0.64 \text{ }\mu\text{s}\))
  • Sync Pulse: 96 pixels (\(3.81 \text{ }\mu\text{s}\))
  • Back Porch: 48 pixels (\(1.91 \text{ }\mu\text{s}\))
  • Total Line: 800 pixels (\(31.78 \text{ }\mu\text{s}\))

The active video period accounts for exactly 80% of the entire \(31.78 \text{ }\mu\text{s}\) horizontal scanline.
