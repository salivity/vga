VGA CRT Monitor Degaussing Coil Explained

A Video Graphics Array (VGA) Cathode Ray Tube (CRT) monitor relies on precise electron beam targeting to produce an accurate image. Over time, internal metal components can become magnetized by ambient magnetic fields, resulting in distorted colors and discolored patches across the screen. The degaussing coil is an internal electromagnetic loop designed to neutralize this unwanted magnetic buildup. It activates automatically at startup to reset the display’s magnetic state, ensuring accurate color purity and a clean visual output.

What Is a Degaussing Coil?

The degaussing coil consists of a thick loop of insulated copper wire wrapped around the perimeter of the CRT's front glass face, just behind the bezel. It is connected directly to the monitor's alternating current (AC) power supply through a specialized thermal component called a Positive Temperature Coefficient (PTC) thermistor.

Inside a color CRT, three electron beams (red, green, and blue) pass through an extremely fine metal sheet located just behind the phosphors on the screen. This sheet is known as a shadow mask, aperture grille, or slot mask. If this metal sheet acquires a static magnetic charge, it bends the incoming electron beams off their intended paths. As a result, a beam intended for a green phosphor might strike a red or blue phosphor instead, producing blotches of incorrect colors—a flaw known as a color purity error.

Why the Coil Activates on Startup

The degaussing coil is triggered at startup to counteract magnetic interference that accumulates while the monitor is powered off or resting in a fixed environment. Several factors cause this magnetization:

  • The Earth’s Magnetic Field: The planet's natural magnetic field constantly acts upon the monitor. Simply rotating the monitor on a desk or moving it to a new location alters the angle of the field, magnetizing the internal mask.
  • External Electronics: Unshielded speakers, power transformers, power strips, and other nearby electronics emit magnetic fields that gradually magnetize the CRT's metallic framework.

Running the degaussing cycle at startup guarantees that each viewing session begins with a neutral, demagnetized shadow mask.

How the Degaussing Process Works

When the monitor is turned on from a cold state, the PTC thermistor offers very low electrical resistance. This allows a massive surge of alternating current from the mains power to flow through the degaussing coil for a fraction of a second.

This sudden current creates a powerful, rapidly alternating magnetic field that penetrates the front of the tube. The alternating field repeatedly flips the orientation of the microscopic magnetic domains within the shadow mask.

As the high current flows through the PTC thermistor, the component heats up almost instantly. As its temperature rises, its electrical resistance climbs exponentially, choking off the current to the coil over the course of one to two seconds. This causes the alternating magnetic field to gradually decay to zero. As the field collapses, the magnetic domains inside the metal settle in random directions, leaving the mask completely neutralized.

This process produces the distinctive loud "thunk," hum, or metallic twang heard when powering on a CRT monitor, accompanied by a momentary shiver or wobble across the displayed image. Once the thermistor is hot, it keeps the current blocked until the monitor is turned off and allowed to cool down, preventing the coil from overheating or interfering with normal operation.
