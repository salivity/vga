How CRT Purity Rings Correct Color on VGA Monitors

Color purity on a Video Graphics Array (VGA) Cathode Ray Tube (CRT) monitor depends on each electron beam striking only its designated colored phosphor dots without striking neighboring ones. Magnetic purity rings, mounted on the neck of the CRT, eliminate color distortion by generating an adjustable magnetic field that shifts the trajectories of the electron beams as a single unit. By altering the position and separation of these magnetized rings, technicians align the origin points of the beams so they pass precisely through the apertures of the shadow mask or aperture grille, ensuring uniform, accurate color reproduction across the screen.

The Mechanism of Color Purity in CRTs

A standard color VGA CRT contains three separate electron guns generating beams for red, green, and blue. Just before the glass faceplate lies a perforated metal sheet—typically a shadow mask or an aperture grille—followed by a layer of red, green, and blue phosphor triads.

For the monitor to display colors accurately, the red beam must strike only red phosphors, the green beam only green phosphors, and the blue beam only blue phosphors. If an electron beam strays and strikes an adjacent phosphor dot, it causes a purity error, visible as blotches of discoloration, tinting, or uneven color patches across the display.

Physical Design of Purity Rings

Purity rings are the rearmost pair of rings in the multipole magnet assembly located on the neck of the CRT, positioned behind the deflection yoke and ahead of the electron guns:

  • Two-Pole Magnetism: Unlike the four-pole and six-pole rings used for static convergence, purity rings are a matched pair of two-pole (dipole) circular magnets.
  • Opposing Polarities: Each ring contains a north and a south pole across its diameter.
  • Adjustable Tabs: Each ring features a protruding plastic tab, allowing it to be rotated independently around the glass neck.

How the Rings Alter the Electron Beams

Purity rings correct beam alignment through magnetic deflection governed by the Lorentz force:

  1. Adjusting Field Strength: When the tabs of the two rings are aligned with opposite poles directly over one another, their magnetic fields cancel each other out, resulting in zero net deflection. Spreading the tabs apart introduces a stronger magnetic field across the neck of the tube.
  2. Adjusting Field Direction: Rotating both rings simultaneously while maintaining their relative spacing rotates the direction of the magnetic field without changing its intensity.
  3. Cluster Deflection: Because the purity rings are positioned where the three electron beams are tightly clustered near the cathode, the resulting magnetic field moves all three beams together in parallel—up, down, left, or right—without altering their convergence relative to one another.

The Alignment Process on a VGA Display

To correct purity on a VGA monitor, the display is first degaussed to remove stray ambient magnetic fields. The green and blue electron guns are often turned off (or a pure red test pattern is displayed via VGA signal), leaving only the red beam active, as red is the most visually sensitive to contamination.

The deflection yoke is typically drawn slightly back, and the technician adjusts the purity ring tabs until the center of the display shows a pure, uniform patch of the selected color. Once the purity rings center the beam cluster relative to the shadow mask apertures, the deflection yoke is pushed forward against the CRT bell to spread that purity uniformly across the entire raster, after which the rings are glued or clamped in place to prevent drift.
