How VGA Derives 31.468 kHz From a 25.175 MHz Clock

The classic 640x480 Video Graphics Array (VGA) standard relies on rigid electronic timing to draw images onto a display. At the core of this standard is the relationship between the pixel clock frequency and the horizontal scanline structure. By dividing the base 25.175 MHz pixel clock by the 800 total clock cycles allocated to draw and reset a single scanline, the display controller establishes the standard 31.468 kHz horizontal sync frequency.

The Fundamental Mathematical Formula

Frequency measures the number of occurrences of a repeating event per second, expressed in Hertz (Hz). To find the frequency of a completed horizontal line (\(f_H\)), divide the master clock rate (\(f_{pixel}\)) by the total number of clock cycles required to complete one entire horizontal line (\(T_{total}\)):

\[f_H = \frac{f_{pixel}}{T_{total}}\]

Plugging in the standard VGA values:

\[f_H = \frac{25,175,000 \text{ Hz}}{800 \text{ clocks}} = 31,468.75 \text{ Hz}\]

Expressed in kilohertz (kHz), this results in approximately 31.46875 kHz (commonly truncated or rounded in specifications to 31.468 kHz or 31.469 kHz). In terms of time, this means each horizontal line takes exactly 31.777 microseconds to complete:

\[T_H = \frac{1}{31,468.75 \text{ Hz}} \approx 31.777 \times 10^{-6} \text{ seconds}\]

Why 800 Clocks per Line for a 640-Pixel Display?

A standard VGA mode displays 640 visible pixels per row, but the electron beam in a Cathode Ray Tube (CRT) monitor—the hardware for which VGA was originally designed—cannot instantaneously jump from the end of one line back to the beginning of the next. The system requires non-visible buffer intervals known as blanking periods.

The 800 total clock cycles per line are broken down into four distinct stages:

  1. Active Video (640 clocks): The visible pixels output to the display screen.
  2. Front Porch (16 clocks): A brief pause after the active display area where the video signal drops to black, preparing the monitor for synchronization.
  3. Horizontal Sync Pulse (96 clocks): A dedicated synchronization signal that commands the monitor's deflection circuits to steer the electron beam back toward the left edge of the screen (horizontal retrace).
  4. Back Porch (48 clocks): A stabilization period that allows the beam to settle into position at the left margin before active video data resumes.

Adding these segments together yields the line total:

\[640 + 16 + 96 + 48 = 800 \text{ clocks}\]

Deriving the 60 Hz Vertical Refresh Rate

The 31.468 kHz horizontal frequency directly drives the overall vertical frame rate. Just as horizontal lines require blanking intervals, the entire frame requires vertical blanking periods to return the beam from the bottom of the screen to the top.

A 640x480 frame includes extra vertical scanlines:

  • Active Display: 480 lines
  • Front Porch: 10 lines
  • Vertical Sync Pulse: 2 lines
  • Back Porch: 33 lines
  • Total Frame Height: 525 lines

Dividing the horizontal line frequency by the total number of lines per frame yields the final vertical refresh rate:

\[\frac{31,468.75 \text{ lines/second}}{525 \text{ lines/frame}} \approx 59.94 \text{ Hz}\]

This 59.94 Hz figure represents the standard nominal "60 Hz" refresh rate that defines modern compatible VGA output.
