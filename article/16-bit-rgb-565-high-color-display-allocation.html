16-Bit RGB 565 High Color Display Allocation

In a typical 16-bit High Color display mode, visual data is encoded using the RGB 565 format, which assigns 5 bits to red, 6 bits to green, and 5 bits to blue for every pixel. This layout fits exactly into a standard 16-bit word (two bytes), providing a palette of 65,536 distinct colors. By structuring the bits this way, graphic hardware maximizes color depth within strict memory limits while aligning with the biological characteristics of human vision.

Bit Layout and Distribution

Within each 16-bit integer representing a single pixel, the individual bits are assigned from most significant bit (MSB) to least significant bit (LSB) as follows:

  • Bits 15 to 11 (Red): 5 bits representing the red channel. This yields \(2^5\), or 32 distinct levels of red intensity (values 0 through 31).
  • Bits 10 to 5 (Green): 6 bits representing the green channel. This yields \(2^6\), or 64 distinct levels of green intensity (values 0 through 63).
  • Bits 4 to 0 (Blue): 5 bits representing the blue channel. This yields \(2^5\), or 32 distinct levels of blue intensity (values 0 through 31).

Mathematically, the total number of displayable colors is calculated by multiplying the discrete levels of each primary color:

\[32 \times 64 \times 32 = 65,536 \text{ colors}\]

Why the Green Channel Receives Six Bits

The decision to allocate the extra bit to green rather than red or blue is rooted in human biology. The human eye relies on rod and cone photoreceptors, with the peak spectral sensitivity of human photopic (daylight) vision centering around 555 nanometers, which falls directly in the green spectrum.

Because humans perceive subtle differences in green hues and brightness variations far more acutely than differences in red or blue, dedicating 6 bits to green significantly reduces visible color banding. This makes the overall image appear smoother and more natural without exceeding the 16-bit bandwidth constraint.

Memory Representation and Bitmasks

To programmatically extract or pack color components into a 16-bit RGB 565 pixel, standard bitwise operations use specific binary masks:

  • Red Mask: 0xF800 (11111000 00000000 in binary) — shifted right by 11 bits to read the 0–31 value.
  • Green Mask: 0x07E0 (00000111 11100000 in binary) — shifted right by 5 bits to read the 0–63 value.
  • Blue Mask: 0x001F (00000000 00011111 in binary) — requires no bit-shift to read the 0–31 value.

When writing to display memory, individual color components are scaled to their target bit-depth, shifted into their respective bit positions, and combined using a bitwise OR operation to create the final 16-bit pixel value.
