DVI-I Connector Pins for Analog VGA RGB

The Digital Visual Interface Integrated (DVI-I) connector is unique because it supports both digital and analog video transmissions within a single interface. To maintain backward compatibility with the Video Graphics Array (VGA) standard, DVI-I incorporates a specialized analog pin cluster. This article details the specific pins responsible for transmitting analog RGB signals and synchronization data, explaining their designated roles in delivering an analog display output.

The Analog Pin Cluster on DVI-I

A DVI-I connector features a distinct block on one side containing a flat horizontal ground blade surrounded by four quadrant pins. These four pins—designated as C1, C2, C3, and C4—along with the blade and a digital pin carry the legacy analog VGA signals.

The specific roles for the pins handling the analog RGB signals are:

  • Pin C1 (Analog Red): Transmits the analog red video signal.
  • Pin C2 (Analog Green): Transmits the analog green video signal.
  • Pin C3 (Analog Blue): Transmits the analog blue video signal.
  • Pin C4 (Analog Horizontal Sync): Transmits the horizontal synchronization pulse required to properly align the analog RGB raster lines.

Complementary Pins for VGA Functionality

While pins C1 through C3 transmit the primary color signals and C4 handles horizontal sync, two additional pins are required to complete the analog circuit:

  • Pin C5 (Analog Ground / Return): The flat horizontal blade that provides the common ground return path for the red, green, and blue color signals.
  • Pin 8 (Analog Vertical Sync): Located in the main 24-pin grid, this pin carries the vertical synchronization signal that dictates the display's refresh rate.

Because pins C1, C2, C3, and C4 transmit the core analog color channels and horizontal timing, a passive DVI-I to VGA adapter can route these lines directly into standard 15-pin D-Sub VGA cables without any active signal conversion.
