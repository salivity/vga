VGA Resolution Explained: 640x480 Pixel Calculation

The Video Graphics Array (VGA) standard defines a display resolution of 640 horizontal pixels by 480 vertical pixels, yielding a total display canvas of 307,200 pixels. This article outlines the mathematical principle behind this calculation, explains how the two-dimensional pixel grid functions in digital display hardware, and demonstrates why this specific arithmetic was foundational to early computing architecture.

The Basic Formula

The total number of pixels on any rectangular display is determined by the standard geometric formula for the area of a rectangle:

\[\text{Total Pixels} = \text{Horizontal Resolution (Width)} \times \text{Vertical Resolution (Height)}\]

For a standard VGA display:

\[640 \times 480 = 307,200\]

Step-by-Step Arithmetic

To understand the calculation mathematically without a calculator, the multiplication can be broken down using the distributive property:

  1. Decompose \(480\) into \((400 + 80)\): \[640 \times (400 + 80) = (640 \times 400) + (640 \times 80)\]

  2. Compute the first product: \[640 \times 400 = 256,000\]

  3. Compute the second product: \[640 \times 80 = 51,200\]

  4. Sum the two intermediate values: \[256,000 + 51,200 = 307,200\]

Alternatively, factoring out powers of ten simplifies the calculation: \[(64 \times 10) \times (48 \times 10) = (64 \times 48) \times 100\] Since \(64 \times 48 = 3,072\), multiplying by \(100\) results in \(307,200\).

The VGA Grid System

In computer graphics, a display does not treat resolution merely as an abstract area, but as an addressable Cartesian grid of discrete picture elements (pixels).

  • Horizontal Axis (Columns): The screen contains 640 distinct vertical columns of pixels numbered from \(0\) to \(639\).
  • Vertical Axis (Rows): The screen contains 480 distinct horizontal scanlines numbered from \(0\) to \(479\).

Because each of the 480 scanlines contains exactly 640 individual addressable pixels, the total memory buffer must allocate space for \(480 \text{ lines} \times 640 \text{ pixels/line} = 307,200 \text{ pixels}\).

Hardware and Aspect Ratio Context

Introduced by IBM in 1987, the 640×480 standard maintained a \(4:3\) aspect ratio:

\[\frac{640}{480} = \frac{4}{3} = 1.333\dots\]

Because cathode-ray tube (CRT) monitors of that era were manufactured with physical dimensions of 4 units wide by 3 units high, a resolution of 640×480 produced square pixels (a 1:1 pixel aspect ratio). This eliminated image distortion when rendering circles and geometric shapes.

At 307,200 pixels, standard 16-color VGA mode (which used 4 bits per pixel, or half a byte) required:

\[307,200 \times 0.5 \text{ bytes} = 153,600 \text{ bytes} \approx 150 \text{ KB}\]

This footprint fit comfortably inside the standard 256 KB video RAM (VRAM) integrated onto IBM VGA adapters, making 307,200 the optimal pixel count for the hardware constraints of the time.
