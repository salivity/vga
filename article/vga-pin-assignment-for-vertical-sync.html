VGA Pin Assignment for Vertical Sync

This article provides an overview of the pin configuration on a standard Video Graphics Array (VGA) connector, identifying the exact pin designated for the vertical synchronization (V-Sync) signal and detailing its function in video transmission.

On a standard 15-pin DE-15 (commonly referred to as DB-15) VGA connector, Pin 14 is assigned to the vertical synchronization signal.

The vertical sync signal is responsible for dictating the display's vertical refresh rate. It delivers a pulse that instructs the monitor to end the current frame scan at the bottom of the screen and return to the top to begin drawing the next frame. Without a functional V-Sync connection, the display will suffer from vertical rolling, tearing, or an inability to acquire a signal lock.

Related Synchronization Pins

The VGA standard organizes synchronization through dedicated signal paths:

  • Pin 10 (Sync Ground): Serves as the ground return path for both horizontal and vertical sync signals.
  • Pin 13 (Horizontal Sync): Carries the horizontal synchronization (H-Sync) signal, which aligns the scan lines from left to right.
  • Pin 14 (Vertical Sync): Carries the vertical synchronization (V-Sync) signal, aligning the refresh cycle from top to bottom.

When looking directly at a female VGA port, Pin 14 is located in the bottom row, second from the right (between Pin 13 and Pin 15). On a male VGA plug, Pin 14 is positioned in the bottom row, second from the left.
