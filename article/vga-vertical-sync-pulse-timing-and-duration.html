VGA Vertical Sync Pulse Timing and Duration

In the Video Graphics Array (VGA) standard, the duration of the vertical synchronization (VSync) pulse is defined in terms of complete horizontal scan lines rather than discrete units of absolute time. For the baseline 640x480 resolution running at 60 Hz, the specification designates the vertical sync pulse width as exactly two horizontal line intervals. This mechanism provides the display hardware with a distinct, measurable timing window to reset the cathode-ray tube electron beam—or internal digital raster counters—from the bottom of the screen back to the top.

Horizontal Line Units in Vertical Timing

VGA relies on a hierarchical timing structure where horizontal timing dictates vertical timing. The master pixel clock (nominally 25.175 MHz for standard 640x480) drives individual pixels and horizontal lines. Each complete horizontal line requires 800 pixel clock cycles, resulting in a horizontal line period (\(T_H\)) of roughly 31.778 microseconds:

\[\frac{800 \text{ pixels}}{25.175 \text{ MHz}} \approx 31.778 \text{ }\mu\text{s}\]

Because vertical timing is measured in horizontal lines, the duration of any vertical interval—including the vertical sync pulse—directly depends on this horizontal scan line frequency (approximately 31.469 kHz).

Frame Structure and Sync Duration for 640x480 at 60 Hz

A standard 640x480 VGA frame consists of 525 total vertical lines partitioned into four sequential stages:

  1. Active Video: 480 lines where image data is drawn.
  2. Vertical Front Porch: 10 lines of inactive blanking before the sync pulse begins.
  3. Vertical Sync Pulse: 2 lines where the sync signal is asserted.
  4. Vertical Back Porch: 33 lines of blanking that allow the raster position to settle before the next active frame begins.

With the pulse duration fixed at 2 scan lines, the physical duration in microseconds is calculated by multiplying the line count by the horizontal line period:

\[2 \times 31.778 \text{ }\mu\text{s} \approx 63.556 \text{ }\mu\text{s}\]

During these 63.556 microseconds, the VSync pin remains asserted, signaling the display to transition between frames.

Polarity and Display Detection

The VGA specification also defines the electrical polarity of the vertical sync pulse. For the 640x480 mode at 60 Hz, both horizontal and vertical sync pulses are active-low (negative polarity). In standard operation, the VSync line idles at a high logic level (+5V or +3.3V depending on the implementation) and transitions to a low logic level (0V) for the exact duration of the 2-line pulse.

Historically, multi-sync CRT monitors used combinations of positive and negative polarities across HSync and VSync to automatically detect the vertical resolution (such as 350, 400, or 480 lines) and adjust the vertical deflection circuits accordingly.

Variations Across VGA-Derived Standards

While the classic 640x480 @ 60 Hz standard sets the vertical sync pulse strictly at 2 horizontal lines, other VESA and extended VGA standards define different durations depending on the refresh rate and total frame size:

  • 640x400 and 720x400 (Text Modes at 70 Hz): Use a 2-line vertical sync pulse with positive polarity on VSync.
  • 640x480 at 72 Hz / 75 Hz: Use a 3-line vertical sync pulse.
  • 800x600 (SVGA at 60 Hz): Uses a 4-line vertical sync pulse over a 628-line total frame.

Regardless of the target resolution or refresh rate, the core design principle remains unchanged: the VGA specification always anchors the vertical sync pulse duration to an exact integer count of horizontal scan lines.
