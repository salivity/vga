What Is a Sync Stripper Circuit in VGA to SCART?

This article explains the function of a sync stripper circuit and why it is a critical component when adapting a Video Graphics Array (VGA) signal for use with an RGB SCART display. It breaks down the fundamental differences between VGA and SCART synchronization methods, details how integrated circuits like the LM1881 isolate timing signals, and outlines why consumer television hardware requires this signal conditioning to achieve a stable picture.

Understanding the Sync Stripper Circuit

A sync stripper is an active electronic circuit designed to isolate and extract timing information—known as synchronization or "sync"—from a video signal that carries other data. In video transmission, sync signals tell a display precisely when to draw each horizontal line and when to refresh the frame vertically.

Commonly built around integrated circuits such as the Texas Instruments LM1881, a sync stripper detects the negative-going sync pulses embedded within a video waveform. It filters out active picture information (luminance and chrominance) and outputs clean, dedicated digital pulses, such as composite sync (CSync), vertical sync (VSync), and burst/back porch timing.

The Synchronization Mismatch: VGA vs. RGB SCART

To understand why a sync stripper is used, it is necessary to examine the architectural differences between standard PC VGA and consumer RGB SCART interfaces.

  • VGA (RGBHV): The standard VGA port carries color data across three separate analog lines (Red, Green, Blue) and divides synchronization across two distinct lines: Horizontal Sync (HSync) and Vertical Sync (VSync). These sync signals operate at TTL (Transistor-Transistor Logic) voltage levels, typically around 5 volts peak-to-peak.
  • RGB SCART (RGBS / RGB with Composite Video): The European SCART standard carries Red, Green, and Blue analog lines, but it does not have separate pins for horizontal and vertical sync. Instead, it relies on a single Composite Sync (CSync) signal fed into Pin 20. SCART televisions expect this signal at a much lower consumer video level, typically 1 volt peak-to-peak into a 75-ohm load (with the sync pulse itself occupying roughly 0.3 volts).

Why a Sync Stripper Is Used in VGA to SCART Adapters

When routing a VGA signal to an RGB SCART television, the separate 5V TTL horizontal and vertical signals must be converted into a compatible format that the television's deflection circuits can decode. A sync stripper fulfills several critical roles in this adaptation process:

1. Extracting Clean Sync from Composite Video

Many custom retro gaming or PC-to-CRT configurations feed a mixed composite video signal or a sync-on-green (SoG) signal to the adapter. If raw composite video is sent directly to SCART Pin 20 alongside the RGB lines, residual image data and color burst information can bleed into the sync channel. This often causes video artifacts such as "sync jitter," image bending at the top of the screen (flagging), or complete loss of horizontal hold. A sync stripper strips away all video information, leaving only a pure, interference-free composite sync signal.

2. Resolving Combined Sync Incompatibilities

When combining VGA's separate HSync and VSync lines using basic passive wiring or simple logic gates (such as an XOR gate), the resulting combined pulse does not always conform to standard broadcast timing intervals (serration and equalization pulses). A sync stripper cleans and regenerates proper timing pulses, ensuring the CRT television can accurately separate the field timings.

3. Protecting Sensitive Consumer Display Circuits

Feeding raw 5V TTL sync from a VGA port directly into SCART Pin 20 can overdrive or damage the input stages of a consumer television designed for standard 1V peak-to-peak signals. Sync stripper circuits act as a buffer, regulating the voltage levels and impedance to meet proper 75-ohm SCART video standards.

Note on Scan Rates

While a sync stripper successfully resolves the sync type and signal integrity issues between VGA and SCART, it does not alter the horizontal scanning frequency. Standard SCART televisions require a 15 kHz horizontal scan rate (standard definition 240p/480i), whereas standard VGA outputs at 31 kHz (480p). Therefore, a sync stripper must be used in conjunction with a video source configured to output a 15 kHz signal.
