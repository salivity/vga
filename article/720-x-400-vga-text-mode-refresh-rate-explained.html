720x400 VGA Text Mode Refresh Rate Explained

This article explores the technical specifications of the classic Video Graphics Array (VGA) standard, focusing specifically on the vertical refresh rate used for its standard 720x400 text mode. It explains the exact refresh frequency, the engineering decisions behind it, and how this mode interacted with early CRT monitors without requiring expensive multi-frequency hardware.

The vertical refresh rate traditionally paired with the 720x400 text mode on standard Video Graphics Array (VGA) displays is 70 Hz (more precisely, approximately 70.087 Hz).

When IBM introduced the VGA standard in 1987 with the PS/2 line of computers, it standardized two primary operating configurations: a 640x480 graphics mode and a 720x400 text mode (often running the standard 80x25 character console).

To keep monitor manufacturing costs low, IBM designed the VGA standard so that fixed-frequency monitors could display both modes without requiring dynamic horizontal sync switching. The horizontal scanning frequency was fixed at approximately 31.469 kHz for both graphics and text.

The timing differences between the two modes were resolved through the vertical refresh rate:

  • 640x480 Graphics Mode: Displaying 480 active lines (out of 525 total lines including vertical blanking) at a horizontal rate of 31.469 kHz resulted in a vertical refresh rate of 60 Hz (pixel clock: 25.175 MHz).
  • 720x400 Text Mode: Displaying 400 active lines (out of 449 total lines including vertical blanking) at that same 31.469 kHz horizontal rate naturally yielded a vertical refresh rate of 70 Hz (pixel clock: 28.322 MHz).

In the 720x400 mode, each character was rendered using a 9x16 pixel matrix across 80 columns and 25 rows (80 × 9 = 720 pixels wide; 25 × 16 = 400 pixels high). Running at 70 Hz provided the added benefit of significantly reducing visible screen flicker on CRT monitors compared to standard 60 Hz modes, making text-heavy environments such as DOS command lines and word processors much easier on the eyes.
