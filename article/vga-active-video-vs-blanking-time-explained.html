VGA Active Video vs Blanking Time Explained

In standard Video Graphics Array (VGA) architecture, video signals are transmitted line by line across a display. Every single horizontal line period is fundamentally split into two distinct operational phases: active video time and blanking time. Active video time represents the exact duration during which visible color data is transmitted to render pixels on the screen, while blanking time is an invisible, non-display interval that allows the display hardware to synchronize and reset its scanning position before drawing the subsequent line.

Active Video Time

Active video time is the operational window where the display controller outputs the actual visual payload. During this period, three separate analog signals (Red, Green, and Blue) vary between 0.0V (black) and 0.7V (maximum brightness) to paint each pixel across the horizontal line.

In an industry-standard 640x480 VGA signal running at a 60 Hz refresh rate:

  • The pixel clock runs at 25.175 MHz (approximately 39.72 nanoseconds per pixel).
  • Exactly 640 pixels are drawn horizontally during the active phase.
  • The total duration of active video time per line is approximately 25.42 microseconds.
  • During this phase, synchronization signals are inactive, and the display panel or cathode-ray tube (CRT) renders the visible image from left to right.

Blanking Time (Horizontal Blanking Interval)

Blanking time, also known as the Horizontal Blanking Interval (HBI), immediately follows the active video phase. Historically required by CRT monitors, this interval turned off (blanked) the electron beam to prevent visible diagonal lines while the beam deflected back to the left edge of the screen—a process known as horizontal retrace. While modern flat panels do not utilize physical deflection beams, digital receivers and FPGA controllers still require this interval to align clock phases and decode line boundaries.

During the blanking interval, the RGB analog signals are driven to 0.0V to prevent any light output. The blanking time comprises three sequential sub-phases:

  1. Front Porch: A brief idle period directly after the active video line finishes (typically 16 pixel clocks or ~0.64 microseconds). It provides a temporal buffer ensuring the display circuitry stabilizes before the synchronization trigger occurs.
  2. Horizontal Sync Pulse (HSync): An active-low pulse that commands the display hardware to initiate the retrace to the left edge (typically 96 pixel clocks or ~3.81 microseconds).
  3. Back Porch: A settling period after the sync pulse terminates (typically 48 pixel clocks or ~1.91 microseconds). The display uses this window to calibrate its black-level voltage reference before the next line of pixel data begins.

In total, standard VGA horizontal blanking lasts for 160 pixel clocks, equating to roughly 6.36 microseconds.

Core Differences Summary

Feature Active Video Time Blanking Time
Visibility Completely visible to the user. Non-visible (electron beam or pixel output turned off).
RGB Signal State Active analog voltages between 0.0V and 0.7V. Suppressed strictly to 0.0V (black level).
Primary Function Renders pixel colors and graphics. Provides hardware reset, line synchronization, and calibration.
Standard Timing (640x480 @ 60Hz) 640 pixel clocks (~25.42 µs; 80% of total line time). 160 pixel clocks (~6.36 µs; 20% of total line time).
Sub-Components Pixel sequence (Pixel 0 to Pixel 639). Front Porch, Sync Pulse, and Back Porch.
