What Is the Native Resolution of VGA Mode 13h?

This article examines the technical specifications of Video Graphics Array (VGA) Mode 13h, focusing on its native pixel resolution, color capabilities, memory architecture, and historical impact on PC game development.

The native pixel resolution of VGA Mode 13h is 320 × 200 pixels.

Introduced by IBM in 1987 alongside the PS/2 series of personal computers, Mode 13h became the de facto standard for PC graphics and video games throughout the late 1980s and 1990s.

Pixel Aspect Ratio

Although the resolution is 320 pixels horizontally by 200 pixels vertically, it was designed to display on standard 4:3 cathode-ray tube (CRT) monitors. Because a 320×200 grid does not naturally conform to a 4:3 ratio, the pixels in Mode 13h are rectangular rather than square, with a pixel aspect ratio of 1:1.2 (taller than they are wide). Artists and developers had to account for this vertical stretching when creating graphics to ensure circles and character sprites did not appear distorted on screen.

Color Depth and Palette

Mode 13h provides an 8-bit color depth, allowing 256 colors to be displayed on the screen simultaneously. These 256 colors are selected from an overall hardware palette of 262,144 possible colors. The VGA Digital-to-Analog Converter (DAC) uses 6 bits each for the Red, Green, and Blue channels (\(2^6 \times 2^6 \times 2^6 = 262,144\)). Developers could reprogram this color palette on the fly to produce dynamic visual effects, such as screen fading, palette cycling for water animations, or simulated lighting.

Memory Layout and Architecture

A primary reason for the popularity of Mode 13h among programmers was its linear memory addressing.

At 320×200 with 1 byte (8 bits) per pixel, a complete screen frame requires exactly 64,000 bytes of video RAM:

\[\text{Total Memory} = 320 \times 200 \times 1\text{ byte} = 64,000\text{ bytes}\]

Because 64,000 bytes is just under the 65,536-byte (64 KB) limit of a real-mode x86 segment, the entire display buffer fits into a single memory block mapped directly to segment 0xA000:0000. Unlike other VGA modes that required complex bit-plane switching and bank manipulation, drawing a pixel in Mode 13h required only a single memory write:

\[\text{Memory Offset} = (\text{Y} \times 320) + \text{X}\]

Historical Significance

The combination of 256 on-screen colors and an uncomplicated linear framebuffer made Mode 13h the preferred platform for classic MS-DOS games, including DOOM, Wolfenstein 3D, and numerous adventure titles from Sierra and LucasArts, establishing it as a milestone in PC gaming history.
