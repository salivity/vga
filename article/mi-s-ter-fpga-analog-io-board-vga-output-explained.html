MiSTer FPGA Analog IO Board VGA Output Explained

This article provides an overview of how the MiSTer FPGA platform generates direct Video Graphics Array (VGA) video using its add-on Analog I/O board. It covers the hardware signal path from the Terasic DE10-Nano, the digital-to-analog conversion process, synchronization signal handling, and the necessary configuration settings required to drive standard VGA displays with zero added latency.

The MiSTer FPGA system relies on the Terasic DE10-Nano mainboard, which houses the Cyclone V FPGA. To output native analog video, the MiSTer ecosystem utilizes an Analog I/O board that plugs directly into the DE10-Nano's 40-pin GPIO headers. When an FPGA core runs, it generates cycle-accurate digital video data within the FPGA logic rather than relying on software emulation or external framebuffers.

The Analog I/O board converts these raw digital video signals into standard analog video using a discrete resistor ladder digital-to-analog converter (often referred to as an R2R DAC). The DE10-Nano routes multiple digital GPIO pins per color channel—typically providing 6 bits per channel for Red, Green, and Blue (18-bit color)—to the resistor network on the I/O board. This passive DAC scales the digital pin states into precisely stepped analog voltages between 0V and 0.7V peak-to-peak, matching the electrical specifications required by the red, green, and blue color lines of the standard DE-15 (VGA) connector.

Alongside the color signals, standard VGA requires separate Horizontal Synchronization (HSYNC) and Vertical Synchronization (VSYNC) signals. The FPGA core generates these timing pulses directly according to the simulated hardware's specifications. The I/O board takes these digital pulses from the GPIO pins, routes them through protective buffers or level-shifting logic to meet 5V TTL standards, and delivers them directly to pins 13 and 14 of the DE-15 port.

Because classic gaming consoles and arcade machines output low-resolution signals with a horizontal scan rate of approximately 15 kHz, they are not immediately compatible with standard PC VGA monitors that require a minimum scan rate of 31 kHz. To resolve this without adding input latency, the MiSTer system uses a hardware line-doubler (scandoubler). This can be toggled per core or set globally in the MiSTer.ini configuration file via the forced_scandoubler=1 parameter. The scandoubler duplicates each horizontal line as it is drawn, shifting the 15 kHz signal up to 31 kHz to satisfy standard VGA CRT and LCD monitors.

By bypassing modern digital processing pipelines and routing the FPGA's native color and sync lines directly through an R2R DAC to the DE-15 port, the MiSTer Analog I/O board achieves pure, zero-lag analog VGA output that matches original hardware behavior.
