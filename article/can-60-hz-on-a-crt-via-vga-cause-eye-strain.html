Can 60 Hz on a CRT via VGA Cause Eye Strain?

Using a Cathode Ray Tube (CRT) monitor connected via a Video Graphics Array (VGA) cable at a 60 Hz refresh rate is a proven cause of significant eye strain, headaches, and visual fatigue. While the VGA connection delivers the analog video signal, the primary culprit is the 60 Hz refresh rate interacting with the physical display technology of a CRT. Understanding how CRT monitors draw images, how the human eye perceives flicker, and how analog signals affect clarity explains why this specific configuration leads to discomfort.

Why 60 Hz Causes Severe Flicker on a CRT

Unlike modern Liquid Crystal Displays (LCDs) that illuminate pixels continuously until a state change occurs, a CRT monitor operates by scanning an electron beam across a phosphor-coated screen line by line. The phosphors glow brightly when struck and rapidly decay into darkness.

At a 60 Hz refresh rate, the entire screen is illuminated and fades 60 times per second. This interval is long enough for the human visual system—particularly peripheral vision, which is highly sensitive to light changes—to detect the rapid drop in luminance. This constant, rhythmic pulsing is perceived as screen flicker. The brain and the muscles controlling the eyes work continuously to compensate for the pulsing light, leading to asthenopia (eye strain), burning sensations, blurred vision, and tension headaches.

The Contribution of the VGA Connection

VGA is an analog interface standard. While the refresh rate is dictated by the display adapter and the monitor's capabilities, the VGA connection can compound eye strain in secondary ways:

  • Signal Degradation: Long, unshielded, or damaged VGA cables suffer from signal attenuation and electromagnetic interference. This introduces "ghosting," ringing, and smearing of text and high-contrast lines.
  • Focus Fatigue: Blurry characters caused by poor analog signal transmission force the ciliary muscles of the eyes to constantly strain to bring text into focus, worsening the fatigue already caused by 60 Hz flicker.

Recommended Refresh Rates for CRTs

To eliminate perceptible flicker on a CRT monitor, the refresh rate must be set high enough that the phosphor decay remains imperceptible to the human eye.

  • 60 Hz: Insufficient for CRT displays; causes severe flicker and eye strain.
  • 72 Hz to 75 Hz: The minimum acceptable threshold for brief usage.
  • 85 Hz: The ergonomic standard for standard CRT monitors. At 85 Hz, the phosphors are refreshed frequently enough that the human eye perceives a steady, solid image.
  • 100 Hz or Higher: Ideal for prolonged usage and motion clarity.

How to Mitigate the Issue

To resolve eye strain on a VGA-connected CRT:

  1. Increase the Refresh Rate: Open your operating system's display adapter settings and raise the refresh rate to at least 75 Hz, ideally 85 Hz or higher.
  2. Lower the Resolution if Bandwidth Limits Apply: If the monitor or graphics card cannot achieve 85 Hz at the current resolution due to horizontal scan rate limits, lower the resolution (for example, from 1280x1024 to 1024x768) to unlock higher refresh rates.
  3. Use Quality Cabling: Ensure the VGA cable is double-shielded, contains ferrite beads, and is as short as possible to eliminate blurriness and ghosting.
