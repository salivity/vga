VGA Horizontal Sync Pin Assignment

On a standard Video Graphics Array (VGA) connector, the horizontal sync signal is assigned specifically to Pin 13. This article outlines the exact pinout details for horizontal sync on the standard DE-15 connector, explains its electrical specifications, and details its operational role in synchronizing analog video displays.

The Horizontal Sync Pin: Pin 13

In a standard high-density 15-pin VGA connector (technically designated as a DE-15 or HD-15 plug), Pin 13 carries the Horizontal Synchronization (H-Sync) signal.

  • Pin Number: 13
  • Signal Name: HSync / Horizontal Sync
  • Signal Type: Transistor-Transistor Logic (TTL)
  • Associated Ground: Pin 10 (Sync Ground)

Physical Location on the DE-15 Plug

A standard VGA female socket or male plug arranges its 15 pins across three rows of five:

  • Top row (Pins 1–5): Primary color signals and grounds
  • Middle row (Pins 6–10): Color return grounds and sync ground
  • Bottom row (Pins 11–15): Data lines and sync signals

Viewing a female DE-15 port from the front (with the wider edge at the top), Pin 13 is located in the bottom row, third from the left (between Pin 12 and Pin 14).

Electrical Characteristics and Function

Horizontal sync coordinates the timing between the graphics adapter (source) and the display (receiver) to ensure that lines of video are drawn correctly on the screen.

  • Voltage Levels: The H-Sync signal operates at standard TTL levels (0 V for low, +5 V for high).
  • Ground Reference: The signal relies on Pin 10 for its dedicated ground reference to prevent noise interference from the high-frequency analog red, green, and blue (RGB) channels on pins 1, 2, and 3.
  • Scan Timing: The H-Sync pulse signals the monitor to end the current scanline, blank the beam, and return to the left side of the screen to start the next horizontal line (horizontal blanking interval).
  • Polarity: VGA horizontal sync pulses can be either positive or negative polarity. The polarity combination between Pin 13 (H-Sync) and Pin 14 (Vertical Sync) historically signaled the monitor to switch between standard display resolutions, such as 640x480, 800x600, or 1024x768.
